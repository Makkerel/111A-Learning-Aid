# Quaternions

Euler angles encode a rotation as three numbers and suffer from gimbal lock. Quaternions replace that triple with a single algebraic object — and give a clean map onto $SO(3)$.

> **Definition (Quaternions):** The quaternions are the $4$-dimensional real algebra
>
> $$
> \mathbb{H} = \{ a + bi + cj + dk : a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{R} \}
> $$
>
> with basis $\{1,i,j,k\}$. Multiplication is determined by
>
> $$
> i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1
> $$

Real scalars commute with everything, but quaternion multiplication does **not**: $ij = k$ while $ji = -k$.

## Conjugation, Norm, and Inverses

For $q = a + bi + cj + dk$:

$$
\overline{q} = a - bi - cj - dk
\qquad
|q| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2}
$$

These satisfy $q\overline{q} = |q|^2$, so every nonzero quaternion has inverse

$$
q^{-1} = \frac{\overline{q}}{|q|^2}
$$

A useful consequence: $|pq| = |p||q|$.

# Unit Quaternions

A **unit quaternion** has $|q| = 1$. Then $q^{-1} = \overline{q}$, and the set

$$
U = \{ q \in \mathbb{H} : |q| = 1 \}
$$

is a subgroup of $\mathbb{H}^\times$. Every nonzero $q$ normalizes to $q/|q| \in U$.

## Rotations by Conjugation

In $\mathbb{C}$, a unit complex $z = e^{i\theta}$ rotates the plane by ordinary multiplication: $w \mapsto zw$.

In 3D the analogous move is **conjugation**. Identify a point of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with a pure imaginary quaternion $r = xi + yj + zk$. A unit quaternion $q$ rotates $r$ by

$$
r \mapsto qrq^{-1}
$$

This preserves length:

$$
|qrq^{-1}| = |q||r||q^{-1}| = |r|
$$

Unlike the complex case, conjugation is insensitive to sign:

$$
(-q)\,r\,(-q)^{-1} = qrq^{-1}
$$

So $q$ and $-q$ define the **same** rotation.

## The Double Cover of $SO(3)$

Recall

$$
SO(3) = \{ P \in O_3 : \det(P) = 1 \}
$$

the group of orientation-preserving rotations of $\mathbb{R}^3$. Each element of $SO(3)$ is one unique rotation.

Sending a unit quaternion $q$ to the rotation $r \mapsto qrq^{-1}$ gives a surjective homomorphism $U \to SO(3)$. Because $q$ and $-q$ map to the same rotation, the map is **2-to-1**: unit quaternions are a **double cover** of $SO(3)$.

> **Takeaway:** Every 3D rotation corresponds to a pair $\{q,-q\}$ of unit quaternions. That double cover is why quaternions can track orientation continuously without the singularities of Euler angles.
