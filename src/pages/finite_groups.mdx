# Finite Subgroups of $SO(3)$

Every finite subgroup of $O(2)$ is cyclic ($C_n$) or dihedral ($D_n$). Passing to 3D rotations $SO(3)$ adds only **three** new groups: $A_4$, $S_4$, and $A_5$ — the rotational symmetries of the Platonic solids.

## Action on Poles

Let $G \le SO(3)$ be finite of order $N$, acting on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^2$ by $g \cdot \mathbf{x} = g\mathbf{x}$.

- **Euler's Lemma:** Every $g \in G \setminus \{1\}$ fixes exactly two antipodal points $\pm\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{S}^2$.
- **Poles:** $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{S}^2$ is a **pole** if $\operatorname{Stab}_G(\mathbf{p})$ is nontrivial. The set $P$ of all poles is finite.
- **Orbits:** $G$ acts on $P$. On each orbit $O_i$, every pole has the same stabilizer size
  $$
  n_i = |\operatorname{Stab}_G(\mathbf{p})| = \frac{N}{|O_i|} \ge 2
  $$

## The Counting Equation

> **Theorem.** If $G \le SO(3)$ is finite of order $N$ with pole-orbit stabilizer sizes $n_1, \dots, n_k$, then
>
> $$
> 2\left(1 - \frac{1}{N}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n_i}\right)
> $$

<details>
  <summary>Proof idea (double counting)</summary>

Count the set $\mathcal{S} = \{(g,\mathbf{p}) \in (G\setminus\{1\}) \times P : g\cdot\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}\}$ two ways.

**By $g$:** Each of the $N-1$ non-identity elements fixes exactly $2$ poles, so $|\mathcal{S}| = 2(N-1)$.

**By $\mathbf{p}$:** Each pole in $O_i$ is fixed by $n_i-1$ non-identity elements, so

$$
|\mathcal{S}|
= \sum_{i=1}^{k}|O_i|(n_i-1)
= \sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{N}{n_i}(n_i-1)
= N\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_i}\right)
$$

Equating and dividing by $N$ gives the theorem.

</details>

## Classification

For $N=1$, $G \cong C_1$. For $N \ge 2$, the left side of the counting equation lies in $[1, 2)$, while each right-hand term lies in $[\tfrac12, 1)$. So there are only $k \in \{2,3\}$ orbits, and (assuming $n_1 \le \cdots \le n_k$) only five integer solutions:

| $(n_1,\dots,n_k)$ | Order $N$ |    Group $G$     | Geometry                          |
| :---------------: | :-------: | :--------------: | --------------------------------- |
|      $(N,N)$      |    $N$    |      $C_N$       | Rotations about one axis          |
|     $(2,2,m)$     |   $2m$    | $D_m$ ($m\ge 2$) | Regular $m$-gon in $\mathbb{R}^3$ |
|     $(2,3,3)$     |   $12$    |      $A_4$       | Tetrahedron                       |
|     $(2,3,4)$     |   $24$    |      $S_4$       | Cube / octahedron                 |
|     $(2,3,5)$     |   $60$    |      $A_5$       | Dodecahedron / icosahedron        |

> **Theorem (Classification).** Every finite subgroup of $SO(3)$ is isomorphic to $C_n$ ($n \ge 1$), $D_n$ ($n \ge 2$), $A_4$, $S_4$, or $A_5$.

> **Takeaway.** Finite rotational symmetries in 3D are the familiar planar ones ($C_n$, $D_n$) plus exactly three Platonic leftovers: $A_4$, $S_4$, $A_5$.
